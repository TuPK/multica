The **Company** page draws your workspace as a company. Squads sit in rooms, rooms sit on floors, and agents sit at desks in their home squad's room. Each agent shows what it is doing, and anything waiting for your decision is marked.

### Floors, rooms and desks

A floor holds six rooms, three across and two down. Floor names are yours; up to 24 floors. Each room has ten desks: the leader sits first, then agents in the order they joined. Human members are not drawn. A squad without a room waits in the "Not on a floor" tray, and **Seat all** places every waiting squad at once.

An agent is drawn at a desk in one squad only, its **home squad**. It can belong to several squads.

### Proposals and decisions

A proposal is a squad's request for your decision. It contains a summary, at least one evidence link, a recommendation, the options considered, cost and risk, and the decision being asked for. Proposals do not notify you on their own. They wait in the **Decisions** queue, and the Secretary's daily brief lists them.

- **Approve**: the proposal is accepted. If it names a squad to take the next step, the company creates a project and a kickoff issue for that squad.
- **Reject**: closes it, and your note is posted as a comment that mentions the proposing leader, so the squad can revise and resubmit.
- **Ask for changes** and **Decide later** are also available.

Only owners and admins decide. Agents, including the Secretary, cannot.

Each squad has limits on pending proposals and on proposals filed in a rolling week, and the workspace holds at most ten pending proposals.

### Goals and metrics

A **goal** says what the company, a floor, a squad or an initiative is working toward in a period. It has **key results**, and each key result follows one **metric**. Progress and confidence are calculated from the metrics, so nobody has to write a status report.

Metrics come from three sources: built-in metrics calculated nightly from the workspace (issues done, cycle time, run success rate, cost, pipeline health), manual readings entered by a member or agent, and webhook readings from outside the product, such as sign-ups or revenue.

Confidence is one of three levels: on track, at risk, or off track, compared with a straight line from the period's start to its end. You can override it with a note.

### Pipeline and initiatives

An **initiative** is one venture: a product, a feature line or a market experiment. It moves through the **pipeline**, a fixed sequence of seven stages, each owned by a squad and each with the artifacts it must produce:

1. **Validate**
2. **Define**
3. **Plan**
4. **Build**
5. **Launch**
6. **Operate**
7. **Learn**

The owning squad attests that a stage is complete, and the initiative advances when its artifacts are in place.

The **Launch gate** is always your decision, at every autonomy level. The **Operate** stage ends with a review gate: continue, scale (open a follow-on initiative at Define) or sunset.

An initiative starts from an approved opportunity, or you can create one directly from the Pipeline page. You can send it back to an earlier stage, shelve it (its stage clock stops), or close it with a reason.

### Daily brief from the Secretary

Each morning the Secretary writes a brief from the company's digest. It contains a headline, the decisions waiting for you, progress by floor, goals that changed, budgets, pipeline movement, people reviews, risks, and notes. It takes about two minutes to read. Rate each brief as useful or not useful; the Secretary adjusts to your ratings.

### Starter company

The **starter company** wizard creates a ready structure: a Secretary, a knowledge librarian, and squads for research, product, build, launch and governance, each with a charter and routines. Running it again creates nothing twice. The starter company starts with a company budget of $500 a month in warning mode.
