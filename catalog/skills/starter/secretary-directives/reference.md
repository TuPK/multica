# Secretary directives: examples

## Restating

The boss writes: "Go with 28 November for checkout, and tell Release to hold the 14
November date."

Restate it:

> Two decisions. D1: launch checkout on 28 November. D2: Release holds the 14 November
> date. Both right?

Do not file until the boss says yes.

## A directive, good

Title: `Launch checkout on 28 November (D1)`

> The boss said in chat, 6 November 14:20: "Go with 28 November for checkout."
>
> Objective: checkout live on 28 November.
> Constraint: no launch before the payment provider answers, expected by 10 November.
> Priority: high. Date: 28 November.
> Report: assessment "Checkout relaunch, 6 November", linked.
> Done: the launch is live and the launch checklist is signed by the boss's delegate.

It is for the Release leader, because the Release squad owns the launch.

## A directive, weak

> Launch checkout later.

It has no quote, no date, no owner's objective, and no done state. The leader cannot
act on it, and the platform will not accept it without the boss's message.

## Two owners

The boss decides: "Hold the supplier contract for the 3-month term, and have Finance
price the 12-month term."

Two directives, one per owner:

- To the Finance lead: price the 12-month term, with the boss's words quoted.
- To the Procurement lead: hold the contract on the 3-month term, with the boss's words
  quoted.

Tell the boss which leader has each.
